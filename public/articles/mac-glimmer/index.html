Fixing a MacBook Keyboard Light That Turns Off When You Log In (After a Screen Replacement)

A glimmer of hope for every MacBook left in the dark.

TL;DR ( I just care about the fix ): If your MacBook’s keyboard light goes dark the second you log in and the brightness keys do nothing ( usually after a screen replacement ), grab Glimmer. It’s a free menu bar app I made that brings the keyboard light and brightness controls back.

👉 Download Glimmer.dmg, open it, drag Glimmer into Applications, and open it. That’s it. ( More install details here. )

OK, picture this. You turn on your MacBook Pro. The login screen comes up and the keyboard is all lit up and looking great. You type in your password, hit enter, and the second the desktop shows up… the keyboard light goes out. Gone. You hit the brightness keys. Nothing. You try the slider on the Touch Bar. Nothing. You open Control Center and drag the keyboard slider. Still nothing.

Yeah. That was my Mac. For years.

A little backstory. I have a 2019 15" MacBook Pro. The original screen started having issues, so I swapped in the screen from my old 2017 MacBook Pro. ( It worked great and I really didn’t wanna buy a whole new Mac. ) The picture was perfect. The brightness controls on the other hand were dead.

( If you don’t care about the nerdy bits and just want your lights back, click here for the fix. I won’t be offended :) )

I tried everything

And I mean everything. If you’ve been googling this you already know the list.

  • Reset the SMC (hold down all the keys until you hear the beep)
  • Reset the NVRAM
  • Boot into Safe Mode
  • Clear the caches, make a new user, reinstall macOS
  • “It’s the ambient light sensor.” “It’s the display cable.” “It’s hardware, just buy a new Mac.”

None of it worked. And the whole time I kept finding other people with the exact same problem all over various forums. Most of those threads end with “put the original screen back” or just… no answer at all. One repair shop even said they saw it on more than 10 replacement screens from different suppliers.

But here’s the part that always bugged me. The keyboard light worked at the login screen! So the light isn’t broken. Something was turning it off on purpose.

So what’s actually going on?

OK, stay with me, this is where it gets a little nerdy. ( I promise it’s worth it. )

Instead of trusting one more forum post, I went digging into what macOS was actually doing on my Mac. The logs, the brightness service, the drivers, all of it. I had Claude Code dig through it with me, which honestly made this go way faster than it would have on my own. Here’s what we found.

1. macOS gave up on the screen. There’s a background service called corebrightnessd that handles every brightness key and slider on your Mac. On my Mac it was failing to set up the built-in screen every time it started up, before I even logged in.

CBDisplayContainer: internal display
CoreBrightness: failed to create DSIICDevice
Failed to create display module for device ID 69733446 … retry

If you ask macOS directly, it straight up tells you the built-in screen “can’t change brightness.” Cool. Thanks.

2. That’s what kills the keyboard light. The keyboard light is run by that same service, and it’s tied to the screen. At the login screen nothing has taken over yet, so the keyboard just sits at a default level and stays lit. The second you log in, the service takes over. But since the screen setup failed, it sets the keyboard light to zero and ignores every key you press. That’s the “lit, then dark” thing. Every. Single. Time.

3. And the real reason: macOS doesn’t recognize the screen. The driver that controls the screen’s backlight has a list of the exact Apple screens it expects for each Mac. My 2019 Mac was looking for a screen called F15Ta031. My 2017 screen shows up as a031, but in that list it’s filed under a different family of Macs. So no match. The driver never gets its brightness settings, the brightness service can’t start, and everything after that goes dark.

So it was never broken hardware. It’s a perfectly good screen sitting in a Mac from a different year, and macOS just doesn’t know what to do with it. That’s also why all of those resets do nothing. The mismatch is still there after every reboot.

The fix

The “right” way to fix this would be to edit Apple’s driver so it knows about the screen. But that means turning off System Integrity Protection and messing with system files, and every macOS update would undo it anyway. Nope. I didn’t wanna do that and I’m not gonna ask y’all to do it either.

So instead I went around macOS. It turns out there’s a little internal “Keyboard Backlight” device that macOS itself uses to turn the keyboard light on and set how bright it is. So I just talked to it directly. The first time I sent it a brightness level the keyboard lit up. After years of it being dark I was way more excited about a row of glowing keys than any grown adult should be. :)

And from there it turned into a real app.

Glimmer

Glimmer is a free, open source menu bar app that brings back your screen brightness and keyboard light controls when macOS has lost them. Here’s what it does:

  • It checks what macOS can still do on its own first. If something works normally, Glimmer leaves it alone and only steps in for the stuff that’s broken. ( So if your Mac is fine, it basically just gives you a nicer slider. )
  • It turns your keyboard light back on and keeps it on. If macOS tries to turn it off again at login or after sleep, Glimmer turns it right back on.
  • Your brightness keys and the Touch Bar buttons work again.
  • It can dim your screen. Since the backlight itself is stuck on, Glimmer dims the picture instead. It never goes fully black either, so you can’t lock yourself out of your own screen. ( Ask me how I know that was important. )
  • It doesn’t change anything in macOS. No kernel extensions, no turning off SIP. Quit it and your Mac is right back to how it was.

How to get it

  1. Download Glimmer.dmg, open it, and drag Glimmer into your Applications folder.
  2. Open it. Since it’s a free app and not notarized by Apple, macOS might block it the first time. If it does, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click “Open Anyway.” You only have to do that once.
  3. Click the ☀︎ in your menu bar. If it asks, let it into Accessibility so your brightness keys work.

That’s it. It works on both Apple Silicon and Intel Macs on macOS 13 Ventura or newer, and it starts up on its own when you log in.

All of the code is on my GitHub, along with a much nerdier write up of everything above if you want the deep dive: https://github.com/adfleshner/mac-glimmer

If your keyboard light is on at the login screen and gone the second you log in, especially after a screen replacement, your keyboard probably isn’t broken and you probably don’t need a new Mac. Give Glimmer a shot.
